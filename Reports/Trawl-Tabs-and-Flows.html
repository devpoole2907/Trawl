Which tabs should the iPhone have?

You asked whether Series and Movies should be separate tabs, what would take a freed slot, and whether Downloads should be first. Behind that sits a bigger worry: changing things forever without reaching a finished state. This page answers the three questions and sets a definition of finished you can hold the app to.

Recommendation
  1. Merge Series and Movies into one Library tab on iPhone, with a Series | Movies switch at the top that remembers your last choice. Switching costs one tap, the same as switching tabs today, so nothing gets slower and a slot is freed.
  2. Give the freed slot to Calendar. It's the screen for your South Park habit (“has the new one aired, and did it arrive?”), and today it's a sheet hidden behind a toolbar button.
  3. Put Library first and Downloads third. Downloads is something you glance at, not where most jobs start any more. Correction: an earlier version said the bar above the tabs shows download progress. It doesn't. It shows notifications, pending approvals and running imports. Only the tab badge counts downloads.
  4. Flatten More so it uses the same sections as the iPad sidebar. Requests and Health each lose a tap, and the app has one map on every device.
  5. iPad and Mac keep separate Series and Movies rows. The sidebar has room, and you already said that's fine.

The sidebar already removed its hub screens. Its own code comment says a hub was “a click spent on nothing”, and that's just as true on a phone. More can draw SidebarSection directly, leaving out whatever is already a tab. The setup-and-configure groups (Integrations, Management, Media Server) can start collapsed, since you visit them rarely. Then there's one list of destinations, and iPhone and iPad can't drift apart.

The worry

How to know when it's finished

Changing things forever usually means there's no target to hit. Here's a target:

  • The jobs table is the scope. A structural change is allowed only if it brings a job within its budget, or adds a job you really do every week.
  • Every job has a journey test that walks its route from launch. Several already exist: Up Next, row long-press, Search long-press and Add Series.
  • A week of real use. Once every job is within budget, use the app for a week and only write down a flow when it gets in your way. If the list is empty, navigation is done, and the remaining work is polish and bugs, which do come to an end.

This proposal is the last structural change it suggests. Everything after it is in-screen polish, tested one job at a time against the table.

Considered and left out

What I'd hold off on

no

A Home or Today dashboard tab

It's tempting, but it would be a new screen that copies Calendar, Downloads and Missing. That's exactly the change-for-its-own-sake you're worried about. Calendar already does most of this job.

later

Choose your own fourth tab

If Requests turns out to be your daily inbox rather than Calendar, a setting could pick which one takes the slot. Add it only after the week of use says you need it.

no

Changing the iPad sidebar

Its sections already work. The only change there is that More on iPhone starts using the same sections.

Cost

What changing it involves

  • A compact-only library tab that shows the existing Series or Movies list. Each keeps its own navigation history, so switching doesn't lose your place. RootTab.series and .movies stay for the sidebar, deep links, Shortcuts and Spotlight.
  • Calendar becomes a tab root on compact screens. The toolbar button that opens it as a sheet goes away.
  • New tab order. The default startup tab becomes Library, but anyone who picked a startup tab in Settings keeps it.
  • More draws its list from SidebarSection, and the four hub screens are removed. More's search index stays the same.
  • UI tests that call openDestination(.series) or (.movies) get routed through the switch. That's one place in the helper, plus new journeys for the switch and the Calendar tab. Then the full test plan runs.
